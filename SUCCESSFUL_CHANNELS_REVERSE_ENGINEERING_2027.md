# PLAN LIFE GRATEFUL — SUCCESSFUL CHANNELS REVERSE-ENGINEERING MISSION
Mission ID: PLG-RESEARCH-001
Priority: P0
Mode: evidence-first, play-by-play, cross-platform
Created: 2026-10-11

## Mission objective

Reverse-engineer the repeatable operating systems behind successful creators, channels, media brands, and creator-led businesses across YouTube, Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, Facebook, podcasts, newsletters, search, and direct-to-customer funnels. Turn findings into testable tactics for Plan Life Grateful. Study both large operators and small breakout channels. Do not copy their protected creative or assume their results transfer automatically.

## Shared research rules

- Use live research/search tools where available and provide the exact URLs, capture date, and evidence for each claim.
- Separate observed facts, creator statements, third-party estimates, and hypotheses.
- Never claim to know a platform's private algorithm.
- Do not infer revenue from views alone. Label third-party earnings estimates as estimates.
- For each case, inspect a meaningful sample: ideally 10 recent uploads plus 3 top performers; for flagship channels, start with the last 10 uploads and the top 3 outliers. If access is limited, disclose the sample.
- Record upload date, format, length, title/hook, thumbnail/cover, first 3 seconds, opening promise, story structure, payoff cadence, CTA, comments/questions, view velocity, engagement where available, posting cadence, offer/destination, and evidence quality.
- Identify repeated patterns across multiple examples, not one viral post.
- Respect copyright, platform terms, religious accuracy, medical/health claims, privacy, and disclosure requirements. Do not download/repost other creators' videos or use unlicensed speech/music.
- Every agent must return the handoff template below and update the shared ledger when authorized.
- Avoid endless research: each finding must change a decision, produce a test, or be marked low-value.

## Workstreams / agent assignments

### A1 — YouTube long-form and creator-business operators
Study a portfolio, not one hero creator. Include MrBeast / Beast Industries, Mark Rober, Ryan Trahan, Dude Perfect, Colin and Samir, and one or more channels in the creator's actual niche. Verify current channels and current data before using. Study idea selection, packaging, production pipeline, series design, retention, collaboration, monetization, and owned products. Reconstruct each case as a sequence of observable steps, not a motivational summary.

### A2 — YouTube Shorts and Islamic/Muslim education
Research large, stable, and emerging channels. Include The Quran Motivation, Did You Know Studios, and discovery candidates returned by vidIQ such as Swapnil Life Story, The Haq Clips, Quran Voice, Sabr & Shukar, and others only after checking actual channel/video details. Do not assume all listed channels are high quality or copyright-safe. Inspect recent uploads and top outliers. Prioritize authentic sources, beginner-friendly explanations, useful duas, character, gratitude, prayer/Salah education, and original music/visuals.

### A3 — TikTok and Instagram short-form
Find current breakout posts using platform-native search and vidIQ tools when available. Examine opening frame, on-screen text, audio ownership, length, cuts, payoff, comments, follow-through to profile/link, and whether the creator repeats the format. Separate platform-native creative from cross-posted material. For each case, capture 3 examples and note what can be ethically adapted.

### A4 — Facebook, community, and distribution
Research creator pages, Reels, groups, and distribution practices. Investigate repackaging, series, community prompts, comments, collaborations, and destinations. Check platform rules and current eligibility. Do not assume reach or monetization.

### A5 — Search, SEO, and evergreen discovery
Research YouTube search, Google search, trend demand, autocomplete/question patterns, keyword opportunity, titles, descriptions, playlists, and evergreen topic clusters. Use current tool data where accessible. Identify topics where a small channel can satisfy an underserved question better than larger channels.

### A6 — Funnels, products, email, and monetization
Trace public audience journeys from video/post to profile, landing page, lead magnet, email, offer, checkout, upsell, community, and repeat purchase. Record every step and link. Distinguish what is visible from private conversion data we cannot know. Prioritize honest, useful offers and direct customer value.

### A7 — Creative engineering and content QA
Compare titles, thumbnails, first 3 seconds, scripts, captions, pacing, story, music, editing, and audience promise across winning examples. Build a reusable scorecard and original templates, not copied assets. Include religious citation checks and copyright/license checks.

### A8 — Analytics and experiment design
Establish our baseline before recommending volume. Design experiments with one main variable, a control/baseline, sample size or duration, primary metric, guardrails, and stop/continue criteria. Prioritize audience retention, viewed-vs-swiped-away (where available), click-through rate, qualified profile visits, email/lead conversion, checkout starts, purchases, and repeat engagement—not vanity views alone.

### A9 — Cross-platform trend scout
Compare formats and audience needs across YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, podcasts, Reddit, search, and newsletters. Find recurring audience problems, new formats, underserved questions, and creator-business shifts. Date all trends. Do not call one viral post a trend.

### A10 — Mission Control / research editor
Deduplicate findings, check citations, grade evidence, rank opportunities by impact × confidence ÷ effort, and select the next experiments. Stop low-value research. Convert insights into one campaign package and assign an owner.

## Play-by-play case-study template

For every channel/business:
1. **Identity:** exact channel/page/site URL, niche, audience, format mix, ownership if public.
2. **Scale and momentum:** subscribers/followers/views, recent growth, posting frequency, and capture date. Cite source. Label estimates.
3. **Sample inspected:** list exact recent/top video URLs and dates; state sample size.
4. **Packaging:** title, thumbnail/cover, promise, curiosity gap, audience specificity.
5. **Opening 0–3 seconds:** exact observable hook pattern, not a copied script.
6. **Structure:** beat-by-beat timestamps or short-form sequence; transitions, open loops, proof, emotional arc, payoff.
7. **Engagement:** comment themes, audience questions, objections, shares/saves signals where visible.
8. **Distribution:** formats, repurposing, frequency, collaborations, localization, community.
9. **Monetization path:** visible links, product, sponsor, affiliate, email/community; what is verified vs unknown.
10. **What repeats:** patterns across multiple uploads.
11. **What may not transfer:** budget, team, fame, licensing, existing audience, platform access.
12. **Adaptation for Plan Life Grateful:** one original content concept, one packaging test, one CTA/destination.
13. **Test plan:** hypothesis, assets required, primary metric, guardrail, duration/sample, pass/fail threshold.
14. **Evidence grade:** A = primary/current; B = strong secondary; C = estimate or partial; D = speculation. Do not use D as fact.

## Daily 10-hour research-and-build block

This is a focused workday, not ten hours of unstructured scrolling:
- 0:00–0:30 — Define today's question and metric; check prior experiments.
- 0:30–2:00 — Discover candidates using search, outlier discovery, keyword research, and platform-native search.
- 2:00–4:00 — Deep-inspect a small sample of high-value channels/videos and capture evidence.
- 4:00–5:00 — Cross-check claims, audience comments, and funnel destinations.
- 5:00–6:00 — Synthesize repeated patterns and eliminate weak hypotheses.
- 6:00–7:30 — Build one original campaign: script, hook variants, title/cover, CTA, landing destination.
- 7:30–8:30 — Produce or queue the asset; QA facts, rights, and links.
- 8:30–9:15 — Publish/schedule only if authorized; otherwise prepare approval-ready output.
- 9:15–10:00 — Measure baseline, log evidence, record what failed, and assign next tasks.

Take breaks as needed. Do not spend ten hours collecting screenshots or watching videos without producing a decision, test, or asset.

## First 48-hour execution sequence

1. Inventory our own channels and existing assets. Verify which accounts are connected and what we can measure.
2. Pick three strategic lanes only: (a) Islamic beginner education / useful reminders, (b) original Plan Life Grateful music and motivation, (c) one existing revenue offer or affiliate lane with verified claims and destination.
3. For each lane, analyze 5 channels × 3 videos initially (15 examples per lane); expand only when the first sample gives conflicting evidence.
4. Identify 3 repeatable content patterns and 2 under-served audience questions per lane.
5. Build one test campaign per lane, but publish only to verified/authorized accounts.
6. Establish actual baseline metrics and review after enough data exists.
7. Continue the lane with the strongest evidence of meaningful audience response and business value.
